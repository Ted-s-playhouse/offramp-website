# FB post — "Why smart real estate investors chase pre-auction foreclosures"

**Why smart real estate investors chase pre-auction foreclosures**

Most investors wait for a house to hit the auction block or show up as a bank-owned (REO) listing. By then everyone can see it — and the margin is already gone.

The smart money works the deal BEFORE the sale. Here's why 👇

1️⃣ **You get the information first.** When you work a property in pre-foreclosure, you already know the whole story — the owner, the debt, the equity, the timeline. You're not guessing off a stale list; you're the most informed person on that property before most people even know it exists.

2️⃣ **You follow it through the entire cycle.** If the owner doesn't sell and it goes to auction — or later becomes an REO — you already have the file. You know the numbers and the property, so you can move the moment it hits the next stage. You track it through the full lifespan of the foreclosure cycle instead of starting from scratch every time.

But here's the part most people miss:

**Pre-foreclosure is the one stage where you can actually help the homeowner.**

There are almost no resources that teach a homeowner what their options really are once they fall behind. Most people facing foreclosure don't know they can sell before the sale, protect their equity, or avoid the credit hit — because nobody ever explained it to them.

An ethical investor can be that resource. You can walk a homeowner through the process, lay out their real options, and help them leave with cash and dignity instead of losing everything at auction. That's not just good business — it's the right thing to do.

Work the pre-auction. Get the information first. And help people while you do it. 🏡

## Infographic concept — "Your options in pre-foreclosure" (homeowner-facing)
A clean 6-tile graphic the investor can share to educate/coach homeowners:
1. **Reinstate the loan** — catch up the past-due amount before the sale.
2. **Loan modification / forbearance** — work with the lender to restructure or pause.
3. **Sell before the sale** — list and sell while you still control the equity.
4. **Sell to an investor for cash** — fast close, walk away with equity, skip the auction.
5. **Short sale** — lender-approved sale for less than owed when there's no equity.
6. **Deed in lieu** — hand the property back to avoid a foreclosure judgment.
Footer line: "Pre-foreclosure is a window, not the end. You have options — and you don't have to figure them out alone."
